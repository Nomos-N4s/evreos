# Decision 0003: The attribution footer on pull request review records is accepted

- **Status**: Decided
- **Date**: 2026-09-02
- **Recorded**: 2026-09-02
- **Deciders**: Founder
- **Cite as**: `decisions/0003`

## Question

Principle I forbids attributing authorship or assistance to an AI or generator
tool anywhere in this repository, and names comments among the places it
reaches. The Development Workflow requires every adversarial review round to be
recorded as a comment on the pull request, and makes a pull request unmergeable
until the most recent round is recorded green there.

The tool that posts those comments appends an attribution footer server-side, to
every comment, with no parameter to suppress it. This is not a choice made at
the keyboard: comments written without a footer arrive carrying one, which was
established by posting three — two review records and a correction whose whole
subject was that the footer breaches Principle I — and finding the footer on all
three afterwards.

So two requirements of this constitution cannot both be met by anyone posting
through that tool. The round must be recorded as a comment; the comment must
carry no attribution; the mechanism supplies one regardless. An implementer may
not resolve that on their own, because either resolution changes what a
NON-NEGOTIABLE principle covers or where a merge gate's evidence lives.

## Decision

The footer is accepted on pull request review records, and Principle I is
amended to say so.

The exception is the mechanism, not the surface and not the content. A footer
that an author could have omitted is a breach exactly as before, wherever it
appears. The exception reaches no commit message, no pull request title or body
and no file in the tree — all of which `scripts/check-commit-hygiene.py` reads
and refuses, and all of which remain refused.

Two alternatives were considered and are recorded as rejected rather than
unexamined. Moving the records into the tree, under `docs/reviews/`, would put
them where the hygiene check enforces Principle I over them; it was rejected
because it moves a merge gate's evidence out of the pull request a reader is
looking at, to solve a footer. Deleting the twelve existing comments was
rejected because the Development Workflow makes a review record append-only:
a record edited or deleted after the fact is void, and deleting twelve would
destroy the audit trail the rule exists to keep.

## Evidence

Twelve comments on pull request #43, ids `5512127095` through `5515274328`, each
ending `_Generated by [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/code)_`. Three of the
twelve — `5514149667`, `5514727652` and `5515274328` — were composed without
one. That is what establishes the footer is appended rather than written, and it
is the whole basis of this decision: without it the right answer would be to
stop writing the footer.

## Serves

- Principle I, by bounding what it forbids.
- The Development Workflow's merge gate, which requires the record to be a
  comment on the pull request and is otherwise unsatisfiable.

## Consequences

Binding from the date above. Review records continue to be posted as pull
request comments and continue to carry the footer.

What replaces the discipline this removes, as the amendment procedure requires:
nothing is withdrawn from the hygiene check, which never read comments and reads
everything it read before. What is withdrawn is a review obligation over one
surface, and it is replaced by this record — an exception nobody can invoke for
anything else, because it names the mechanism and the mechanism is checkable.

What reopens this: the posting tool gaining a way to suppress the footer, at
which point the exception has no subject and the prohibition applies again
without amendment.

## Corrections

None.
